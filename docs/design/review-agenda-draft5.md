# Spec v0.5 review agenda (generated 2026-09-13)

**Overall:** Draft 5 is structurally sound on its pillars but under-specified in the sim rules that decide matches: power shortfall has no end state (the only blocker), and reach, sightings, kill credit, beacon death, money timing and match end are each missing a rule an implementer would need. The new v1 simplification retires about a fifth of the findings outright — everything hanging off MCP, SDKs, LLM seats and the opponent-session launcher — and makes several more one-line edits during the draft-6 rewrite. The remaining agenda splits cleanly into design rules to settle (items 1-21), build-plan ordering and checkpoint criteria (items 22-26), and a short editorial tail; the scope-cut candidates worth deciding first are Dispatches, watch mode and art/audio.

Stats: raw 188, unique 143, confirmed 134, rejected 9, items 30

## 1. Power shortfall: dormancy, the core turret, the priority knob [blocker] (§4, 5, 7, 9)

**Evidence:** L302: dormancy "pauses its operator and fabricator. Structures stay"; and "A key-core always powers its own beacon, so only extra draw (its fabricator, structures and units) can brown it out." L358: a non-core key-core "outputs exactly its own beacon's base draw (net zero)". L366/L360: the Quartermaster "holds fabricator orders, then applies the brownout order" — fabricators are already stopped, so dormancy frees almost nothing, and units, which "draw kW while fielded" (L304), are never shed. L297: the core's autocannon "draws from the core surplus", while L361 spends that same surplus on the unit ceiling. L368 calls the knob "the one player lever", but only L302/L360 use priority. Nothing says a vent takes one Generator (L200, L425).

**Why it matters:** After a lost Generator or core, the shed order can run to its end with the deficit intact and no defined end state — something a 20 Hz deterministic sim must answer every tick. The same gap decides whether the anti-rush turret can go dark, and whether $ converts straight into army size.

1. **The beacon is the unit of power (RECOMMENDED)** — Dormancy powers down everything homed to that beacon — fabricator, its structures and its bound units (they park where they stand and draw 0 kW); the seal, the sphere, and interfacing or recycling stay lit, because the key-core covers those. The core autocannon runs straight off the deep bore, outside the grid, and is never shed (grid supply stays exactly core surplus + Generators). The knob is kW-only: it sets brownout order and nothing else; $ goes by the urgency ladder, then round-robin inside a band. One Generator per vent, output = the vent's richness. Revive only when supply exceeds draw by a margin, so domes do not flicker. The shed loop now always terminates: everything except seals and the bore-fed turret is sheddable.
   *Downside:* One Generator kill can freeze a whole wing mid-Push with no counterplay inside the sealed segment — a big, sudden swing to balance — and parked units need their own state and art. "One per vent" is a count limit, so it needs its story sentence (the vent's heat is the limit, not the tap).

2. **Shed units before domes** — Dormancy stays as written (operator + fabricator). The Quartermaster gains one step between fabricators and dormancy: it mothballs fielded units, lowest-priority beacon first and newest unit first, until supply balances; only then do domes go dark. Turret off the bore, knob kW-only, one Generator per vent, all as in option 1.
   *Downside:* Two shedding mechanisms to design, tune and show in the UI, and "domes going dark" stops being the readable signal of a shortfall because units wink out first.

3. **Write down the literal reading** — Smallest edit. Dormancy also stops the beacon's structures and its bound units, so it frees a known block. The core turret stays inside the grid and browns out with the core, last. The priority knob does both jobs: it breaks ties for $ inside an urgency band and sets the brownout order. A vent caps total output, so extra Generators on one vent buy redundancy and a faster ramp, not more kW.
   *Downside:* The anti-rush guarantee becomes conditional on your own spending — overbuild, or lose a Generator seconds before a rush, and your core's gun goes dark; and a knob with two meanings is more to explain and invites tuning visits, which is the accounting the economy principle wants to avoid.

4. **No dormancy at all: the grid just dims** — Delete the shed order and the knob. A deficit slows everything on the grid in proportion to the shortfall — fabrication, unit speed and fire rate — until supply returns. Turret off the bore; one Generator per vent.
   *Downside:* Loses the dark-dome spectacle the Pall story is built on, replaces a readable event with a continuous efficiency multiplier nobody can see, and makes combat outcomes depend on a hidden percentage.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 answers all four questions with one rule — the beacon is the block of power that goes dark — so the shed loop provably terminates, the grid arithmetic stays uniform (core surplus + Generators, no hidden reservation), and the turret guarantee the "no time-based protection" decision rests on cannot be spent away. It also matches the decisions log, which already says the turret is "powered by its deep bore" (log line 833).

**Spec edits:** Section 5 "Dormant": a dormant beacon powers down its fabricator, its structures and its bound units; seal, sphere, interfacing and recycling stay; it revives once supply exceeds draw by a margin. Section 5/7: the core autocannon runs off the deep bore, outside the grid. Section 7: priority is kW-only; $ by urgency, then round-robin. Section 9: one Generator per vent, output set by vent richness.

## 2. What a sphere is, and what enemy overlap blocks [major] (§2, 5, 7, 8, 10, 19)

**Evidence:** "Sphere" is never defined. Its only introduction is a term-table row, L229: "Beacon · sphere / Stake · holding". It then gates everything: L298 "Each new beacon must be within the commander's range (Tuning) and touch or overlap one of your own spheres. Deploying takes 12 s."; L300 "Where enemy spheres overlap, neither side can build or dig. Interfacing is still allowed."; L358 "All your spheres form one grid"; L380 "one Resonance Spire per sphere". The only radius hint is L390 "A larger beacon radius". §19's Tuning list (L801) names "placement range" but no sphere radius; the decisions log still lists beacon "radius ... overlap between own and enemy spheres" as open (log L496) while keeping the ban (log L516).

**Why it matters:** Placement legality, the single power grid, the spire limit and the overlap lockout are all unimplementable without it, and the ban plus free placement makes a cheap spite beacon freeze the richest central vents — the fight the map generator is built around.

1. **RECOMMENDED — Define the sphere in one sentence, and delete the enemy-overlap rule** — Add as the first row of §5: "A beacon's sphere is the ball of voxels within its radius (Tuning); a Resonance Spire enlarges its own beacon's sphere." Restate placement as: the new beacon's site must lie inside one of your own spheres and within the commander's placement range (Tuning, a separate distance from the ~4-voxel interface range); the commander stays in range for the full 12 s, and death, leaving or illegal placement aborts with a full refund. Then delete the "Enemy overlap" row entirely: build, dig and deploy are legal anywhere, and combat decides who keeps what. One line replaces it in §7: a vent takes one Generator, held by the first to finish.
   *Downside:* Contested vents become a build race with structures rising under enemy fire, which may read as chaos on camera, and the vent-occupancy line is a new technical rule needing its story sentence. It also removes the rule's unstated job of stopping an enemy walling you in, which now has to be answered by fighting.

2. **Define the sphere, keep the ban, and say deploying is not building** — Same definition sentence, but the overlap row stays and gains: placing a beacon is not building, so forward deploys into enemy-covered ground are legal; repair of your own existing structures is also exempt from the ban.
   *Downside:* The denial play survives — a net-zero-power spite beacon still costs only $ and 12 s and freezes all construction in the overlap — and a forward beacon that can build nothing is a confusing object to hand the player. Two more special cases in the rule that was meant to be simple.

3. **Define the sphere, keep the ban, and make enemy-covered ground illegal to deploy into** — Same definition sentence; import the design doc's existing E0420 clause ("inside a contested zone") into §5 so placement is checked at deploy start and at completion, aborting with a full refund.
   *Downside:* Makes first-mover denial the dominant opening: whoever reaches a rich central vent first with a cheap beacon freezes it for both sides and cannot be built out, only shot out — and the seat that wants the ground least wins every standoff.

4. **Define the sphere, and price contested ground instead of banning it** — Same definition sentence; building and digging in overlap is allowed but slower and dearer (competing Ledger claims), with the story line the caps rule requires.
   *Downside:* A new tuning number and extra frontier state for a corner case, against "simpler is better", and a terrain-editing duel that is hard to read on screen.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 is the only one that removes scope: it deletes a rule, a contested-zone state and a verifier case instead of adding special cases, and it restores the fight over the central vents the map generator exists to create.

**Spec edits:** §5: new first row defining sphere and radius (Tuning); placement row reworded to site-inside-your-own-sphere plus a named placement range, commander present for the 12 s, abort refunds in full; delete the "Enemy overlap" row. §7: one Generator per vent. §8: "one Resonance Spire per beacon". §19: add sphere radius to Tuning.

## 3. "Reach" means several different ranges [major] (§1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 11, 16, 17)

**Evidence:** One word carries four ranges. L202 (caps rule): "A beacon's reach is where it can see or relay orders" — beacon-scoped. L335 (Attack): "Reach: anything the seat sees or recently saw: its sphere, plus the live vision of its units and structures ... plus Sensor Spire reveals and Radio Mast relay coverage" — seat-wide. The same word then sets salvage range (L264, L301; L333 "salvage wrecks and ruins within reach") and bodyguard range (L334 "Guards escort the commander ... while it is in this beacon's reach"). Read seat-wide, drones salvage and guards escort map-wide. Separately L393 says v1 radio has "no line-of-sight checks, relays or jamming", yet relay coverage is a reach input at L334, L335, L388, L515 and slice 4 content at L738.

**Why it matters:** Salvage and escort range decide drone travel economics and whether commander hunting is playable; the verifier checks Attack targets "within reach" (L515), so implementers need one meaning. Today two engineers build sphere-bounded work and map-wide work, and both readings are defensible.

1. **RECOMMENDED — two words: sphere for physical work, reach for knowledge only; drop relay coverage from v1** — "Sphere" is the only range for anything physical: build, dig, salvage, guard escort, placement anchor. "Reach" means only what the seat knows: its spheres, the live vision of its own units and structures, Sensor Spire reveals, within memory N. Relay coverage leaves every v1 reach definition (v1 has no relays), and the Mast's v1 job is messaging alone: operator threat/help messages, go-codes, Dispatches. Live retargeting reads the same seat reach as plan time, so there is one reach and one verifier check.
   *Downside:* Ruins outside every sphere stay unrecoverable until someone expands, so some salvage income disappears; the Mast loses its "extends reach" selling point and rests on go-codes and Dispatches; about eight sentences change across seven sections.

2. **Keep one seat-wide reach everywhere, as written under Attack; only delete the relay wording** — Reach is seat knowledge in every use: Attack targeting, Defend pursuit, salvage and escort alike. One definition, one word, one verifier check, no glossary growth.
   *Downside:* Reclaim drones cross the map to distant ruins and bodyguards leash the commander map-wide, which kills the "your spheres are your footprint" feel and makes drone travel time the dominant hidden cost — accounting the player cannot see.

3. **Keep one word, two scoped definitions: order/sight reach (seat-wide) versus work reach (sphere plus own unit vision)** — State both in §1 and tag every existing sentence with the one it means; the verifier applies the right definition per predicate.
   *Downside:* Two definitions to document, teach, verify and keep in llms.txt for a mechanic that should be one idea; every reader must remember which use is which, and the mis-read stays available.

4. **Minimal edit: rename "relay coverage" to "mast coverage" everywhere and leave the salvage and escort uses alone** — Fixes only the v1-has-no-relays contradiction (§8, §11, §16 P7, §17 slice 4) and reserves "relay" for the roadmap Horizon model.
   *Downside:* The real ambiguity survives untouched: salvage range, escort range and live retarget scope stay undefined, and mast coverage remains a redundant reach input because seat-level reach already covers it.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 removes a concept rather than adding one: the spec already has "sphere", physical work is naturally local, and deleting relay coverage settles the L393 contradiction and shrinks the verifier's reach inputs to things v1 actually has.

**Spec edits:** §1 L202: reach is what a seat sees; sphere is where its drones work. §3 L264, §5 L301, §6 L333: salvage "within the beacon's sphere". §6 L334: guards escort within the sphere; pursue ends at sphere, unit vision or Spire reveal. §6 L335, §11 L515: drop relay coverage; add "live retarget uses the same reach". §8 L388: Mast = messaging; cut peer links. §16 L712, §17 L738: "mast coverage".

## 4. Sighting freshness: window, clock, and when it is checked [major] (§6, 10, 11, 19)

**Evidence:** An Attack target must have been "seen within the last N minutes" (§6 l.335, Tuning), but the same row lets a wave "launch when any of force size, segment time or broadcast go-code (needs a Radio Mast) holds" — up to 8 minutes after the seal. §11 l.515 checks only that "Attack targets lie within reach at plan time"; nothing says the window is re-tested at launch. §6 l.336 implies it is live during the Push: Survey scouts "re-check the sightings closest to leaving the Attack reach window". No clock is named for a sighting's age. §19 l.803 leaves N unset; the only number in the spec is §10 l.460's example, "the nearest known enemy beacon seen within 15 min", never tied to N, and §10 l.469 lets conditions declare their own freshness (KNOWN_WITHIN ms).

**Why it matters:** Two readings of one rule give different answers to "does this attack fire?" for identical play, and they decide whether slice 2 needs a runtime launch gate at all. It also sets whether Survey is a planning choice or a per-Push refresh chore, and whether one example number contradicts the rule.

1. **RECOMMENDED - Plan-time check only, sighting pinned at seal; age runs on match game time** — Reach freshness is a qualification test at plan time and nothing else. When the plan is sealed the target is pinned, and the launch condition never re-tests it; if the target has moved or died, the existing rule applies - "if the remembered target is gone on arrival, the after-action or retarget rule decides" (§6 l.335). State once that a sighting's age accrues on match game time, accumulated across Pushes, and that the Lull and recap add nothing (the snapshot is already frozen at segment end, l.272, and wall-clock time is lint-banned, l.678). Fix the §10 example to read "within the reach window" and let Attack target selectors take their freshness from N instead of declaring their own. No new runtime code, no new diagnostic, one staleness number.
   *Downside:* A wave can leave on information up to N plus one Push old, so timing a Survey sweep late in a Push banks a round of attack legality; baiting and going dark are punished at arrival rather than at launch, and the age chip in the editor reads as a qualification number, not a live guarantee.

2. **Re-test freshness when the launch condition fires** — When force size, segment time or the go-code fires, compare the sighting's age to N; if it is stale, fall through to the retarget rule, else to after-action. Machinery already specified, and the staleness warning family W07xx (§11 l.540) can flag a plan whose latest launch time is likely past the window.
   *Downside:* Adds a runtime gate and a feed event to slice 2: a sealed plan can now contain an attack that simply never launches, driven by a number the player cannot watch move during the Push, which is exactly the kind of invisible accounting the design avoids.

3. **Pin the maximum launch delay to the sighting's remaining freshness** — The verifier rejects (or warns on) an Attack whose latest possible launch time falls outside the target sighting's remaining N, so anything that launches is fresh by construction.
   *Downside:* Couples two tuning numbers (reach memory N and launch timing) that are hard to show together in the editor, and produces a submit-time error for a launch that may never happen; go-code launches have no bounded time at all, so they either escape the rule or become unusable.

4. **Drop N: reach is anything the seat has seen this match, age is informational only** — Remove the reach-memory window entirely. Attack can target any position the seat has ever seen; the age shown in the UI and API informs the player and the Survey program's scan order, and nothing gates on it. One fewer tuning number, one fewer concept.
   *Downside:* Deletes the intel-decay counterplay and feeds the perfect-intel snowball the spec already lists as a risk (l.342, l.794), and it removes Survey's main reason to exist, since refreshing sightings would no longer change what is legal to attack.

**Recommendation:** One clock, one check, no new runtime gate: the counterplay stays in the world (the wave arrives and finds nothing) rather than in a hidden legality test during the Push, and it is the only option that removes code from slice 2 rather than adding it.

**Spec edits:** §6 Attack and §11: reach is tested at plan time only; the target is pinned at seal, and arrival's after-action or retarget rule handles a moved or dead target. Add: sighting age accrues on match game time across Pushes; Lull and recap add nothing. §6 Survey: scouts refresh sightings before the next Lull. §10: example reads "within the reach window"; Attack target selectors take freshness from N. §19: N is in game-time minutes.

## 5. Do Dispatches survive v1 at all? [major] (§1, 2, 3, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13, 14, 17, 18, 19)

**Evidence:** Section 14, line 649: "It never reads incoming Dispatches, and the lobby labels the seat 'built-in'." Section 8, line 405 repeats it: "It never reads incoming text." The only seat that could ever read one is an LLM seat through get_dispatches (line 581), which the new direction moves to v1.2 with slice 9. So no v1 seat can read a Dispatch. The human's editor box (621), the plan's dispatch field (436), get_dispatches (555), the mast hearing gate (400) and the untrusted-text rules (406, 576) serve zero readers. Only the built-in's outgoing flavour lines (649; slice 5, line 739) reach anyone — one-way, at the human.

**Why it matters:** Bluffing needs a reader: "The Ledger enforces no promises, and bluffing is allowed" (403). With LLM seats at v1.2, v1 would ship a two-way channel with no second ear, plus anti-collusion and untrusted-text rules for opponents that cannot collude. Cutting it thins slice 4 and shortens draft 6.

1. **RECOMMENDED — Cut the player-facing Dispatch subsystem; keep a one-way engine chatter ticker** — Delete the plan dispatch field, the editor box beside Submit, get_dispatches, the dispatches[] feed entry, the mast gate on hearing, the untrusted-text security rules and the Dispatch length tuning item. Keep only what already works in v1: built-in seats emit short engine-authored template lines from intent and events, shown as the Push-start ticker, in the recap and in recordings. Frame it in-world as intercepted open-air chatter. Reserve the plan field number so v1.2 re-adds player Dispatches with LLM seats, no major version. Slice 4 becomes "radio" (mast coverage, relay, Sensor Spire reach, go-codes, P7); slice 5 keeps the flavour lines.
   *Downside:* The human still cannot answer back, so the channel is pure atmosphere, and it must be framed as intercepted chatter or it reads as a feature that half-works. Two Decided lines (lore 222, caps example 204) and the roadmap entry (766) need rewording in draft 6.

2. **Cut Dispatches entirely, ticker included** — Remove every trace from v1: section 8's Dispatch table, the lore bullet (222), the caps-rule example (204), section 3's "Seats talk only through Dispatches" (270), section 6's line (343), the API and editor hooks, and the slice 4 and slice 5 line items. Radio in v1 means only what the mast mechanically does: operator-to-operator threat and help messages, commander go-codes, and relay reach. Player Dispatches enter the roadmap alongside directed and encrypted notes, returning with LLM seats.
   *Downside:* Loses a cheap spectacle beat at the Push start and the only seat-to-seat colour in a game sold on being watchable; the built-in's silence makes opponents feel less alive during the fun probe, and the subsystem is rebuilt from nothing in v1.2.

3. **Keep Dispatches as specified and give the built-in operator a small deterministic reader** — A closed vocabulary (threat-at, stay-clear, truce-offer, target-call) parsed out of the text and mapped onto weights the operator already scores: target spread, threat, expansion direction. Free text is ignored. This is the only option under which the human's box does something in v1, and it makes the bluffing claim at line 403 true in the default lobby.
   *Downside:* Adds scope and a parsing plus determinism surface in the same pass that is removing scope, is farmable once a player learns the mapping (false calls steer the opponent), and buys a diplomacy layer that nothing before the fun gate is set up to test.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 removes the whole two-way subsystem and its security and collusion rules while keeping the one piece that already functions in a v1 match, the built-in's outgoing line, so the Push-start ticker survives at near-zero cost and the seam is reserved for v1.2.

**Spec edits:** Section 8: replace the Dispatch table with a short "Chatter" row (engine-authored built-in lines, Push-start ticker, recap, recordings). Delete the dispatch field (10), editor box (13), get_dispatches and dispatches[] plus the untrusted-text rules (12), the caps example (1), the length tuning item (19). Reserve the proto field. Slice 4 becomes "radio"; section 18 gains player Dispatches at v1.2.

## 6. Target spread steers the built-ins away from each other [major] (§3, 14, 19)

**Evidence:** §14 line 647: "the planner scores targets partly by how much pressure the other AI seat is already putting on them, so two AIs don't pile onto the same seat by default." The default match is "one human plus two AI seats" (§3 line 257). The utility terms carry no standings weight ("defence + economy + expansion + capability + attack − risk", line 634). So the human never faces both built-ins, and can farm the weaker one for audit credit ("build cost of enemy assets you destroyed", line 261). §19 line 806 lists "Kingmaking and farming the weakest seat" as a fun-gate risk — but target spread ships in slice 5, wk 43.5 (line 739), after the fun gate at wk 35.5.

**Why it matters:** The asymmetry sits in the configuration nearly every player will play: only an AI can be ganged up on, only the human can farm freely, and nobody converges on a turtling leader. Both scheduled measurements (fun probe wk 17.5, fun gate wk 35.5) run before the rule exists.

1. **Make target spread seat-blind: count pressure from any other seat, not just the other AI seat** — One word changes in §14 line 647: "any other seat" replaces "the other AI seat". A built-in that sees the human already beating on a seat scores that seat lower, so the human's private farm closes. No standings input, no new term, no extra accounting; the rule keeps its original intent (built-ins not wasting effort on one target) and actually loses a special case, since the operator no longer distinguishes human seats from AI seats when scoring.
   *Downside:* Aggression now invites a second attacker: if the human pounds one built-in, the other's spread score pushes it toward the human. That is symmetric and player-caused, but it can still read as "the computer teamed up on me". The trailing seat also dies faster, shortening matches, and nothing yet steers anyone at a turtling leader.

2. **Delete target spread from v1 entirely** — Cut the rule. Targeting falls out of the ordinary utility terms (nearest, weakest, best opportunity). One fewer special case in the built-in operator's scoring and one fewer item in slice 5, which suits a draft-6 narrowing to a rudimentary templates-plus-scoring operator. The §19 risk row then measures genuinely emergent behaviour.
   *Downside:* Both built-ins run the same Balanced weighting (line 634) on a 3-way rotationally symmetric map, so identical scores make convergence likely rather than rare: the default match can routinely become two-on-one against the human, and the deleted rule is exactly the guard that prevented it.

3. **Weight target attractiveness by the live standings, so any seat — human included — becomes a more attractive target as it leads** — Adds a standings term to the §14 utility score, so the built-ins converge on whoever the final-audit standings say is winning. This is the only option that also fixes the turtling-leader gap, since the score already runs live (line 261).
   *Downside:* A third standings dial on top of BMI scaling (line 355) and the award fund's rank multiplier (line 356) — more score accounting for the same catch-up job — and two built-ins converging on a leading human is kingmaking from the other direction: the computer punishing the player for winning.

4. **Leave the rule as written and measure it at the fun gate as planned** — No spec change. The §19 risk row (line 806) already names kingmaking and farming the weakest seat, with the fun gate and playtests as the checkpoint.
   *Downside:* Neither scheduled measurement can see it: the fun probe (wk 17.5) and the fun gate (wk 35.5) both run before target spread ships in slice 5 (wk 43.5), so the gate judges a game that the shipped rule then changes, in the one configuration nearly every player experiences.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 removes a special case instead of adding a term: "any other seat" is strictly simpler than "the other AI seat", it closes the human's free farm without introducing a standings input, and catch-up stays where it already lives (BMI scaling and the award fund's rank multiplier).

**Spec edits:** §14 line 647: replace "the other AI seat" with "any other seat"; add that there is no standings term — catch-up stays in BMI and the award fund. §19 line 806: move the checkpoint to the nightly league (the rule ships slice 5, after the fun gate); observable = share of damage aimed at the trailing seat. Vocabulary: planner → built-in operator.

## 7. Match end at the tick: eliminations, mutual loss, quitting [major] (§3, 19)

**Evidence:** Section 3 gives the win condition but never says when it is evaluated: "The match ends when one seat remains, or at the host-set round limit" (258; summary row 254). Elimination is tick-level — "When a seat's last beacon falls, its units disband on the spot" (264), decisions log 576 "eliminated that tick" — yet nothing says whether the running Push halts at that tick or plays out. If the last two seats lose their last beacons on the same tick, zero seats remain and no rule covers it. Separately, 265: "An eliminated human becomes a spectator ... and can end the match at any time. Standings freeze at that tick and the recording is saved" — never saying whether a winner is declared. The open item at 787 ("final-audit tie-break ... Not yet discussed") resolves none of this.

**Why it matters:** These are the win conditions, and a deterministic sim must answer them identically on every OS. As written, an eliminated player can end a two-AI duel at a moment of their choosing — a one-click kingmaking lever stronger than anything in the score the spec already flags as a risk (806).

1. **One tick rule: end conditions checked every tick; survivor wins; no survivor, the audit decides; the human's button becomes "Abandon match (no winner)"** — The Push halts at the first tick where fewer than two seats remain, standings freeze there, the recap plays. Last seat standing wins. If the last two fall on the same tick, the final audit at that tick decides (destroyed-value credit never decays, so it still separates them) — no draw state, no new UI. "The final tick" is defined as the last tick of the last Push, before the recap, matching 272 ("planning snapshot is frozen at segment end") and 442. The eliminated human keeps a full-map spectator view; their control is relabelled "Abandon match (no winner)": the recording is saved, no result is recorded, and the default is to keep watching.
   *Downside:* Cutting the Push mid-movie loses a little spectacle, and an eliminated player who abandons never learns who would have won. Needs one mid-Push halt path in the runner.

2. **Evaluate end conditions only at segment boundaries, so the Push always plays out** — Simplest runner: one check per segment, no mid-Push halt. A mutual elimination is then just "zero seats at segment end", resolved by the audit the same way.
   *Downside:* Up to eight minutes of a one-seat movie after the match is already decided — the worst case for the biggest unmeasured risk in the project (807).

3. **Halt at the deciding tick, but record a same-tick mutual elimination as a draw** — Same as option 1 for the common case; the zero-seats case is a declared draw rather than an audit result.
   *Downside:* A draw needs its own recap line, standings state and a rule about whether the audit still runs — new UI for a case that will almost never occur.

4. **Keep the eliminated human's power to end the match, defined as running the final audit at that tick and declaring a winner** — No change to the control; it simply means "score it now". Answers the player's curiosity about who was ahead.
   *Downside:* A dead player picks the winner by choosing the moment — exactly the kingmaking already listed as a risk, now one click away.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 answers all three questions with a single rule, adds no new state (no draw, no kingmaker, no headless continuation), and reuses the final audit that already exists instead of inventing a second end-of-match path.

**Spec edits:** Section 3 "Match end" (258): end conditions are evaluated every tick; the Push halts at the first tick where fewer than two seats remain; the last seat standing wins; if none survives that tick, the final audit at that tick decides. Define "the final tick" as the last tick of the last Push, before the recap. Line 265: rename the control "Abandon match (no winner)" — recording saved, no result, keep watching is the default. Line 787: drop the zero-seats case from the open tie-break item.

## 8. Damage-split kill credit: edge cases, remainder, visibility [major] (§3, 5, 7, 15)

**Evidence:** Line 261 is the only kill-credit rule: "Credit for each destroyed asset is split between seats in proportion to the damage each dealt since it was last at full HP, so a last hit steals nothing"; the standings show only each seat's total. Undefined: self-inflicted or unattributable damage, overkill, units that "disband on the spot" and structures that "become neutral ruins" (264), an eliminated seat's accrued share, and repair back to full — Build beacons field "Repair drones [that] fix its structures" (333) — which by the wording erases every share. Decisions log 2.6 item 4 (line 847) requires "deterministic remainder handling"; the spec never states it. The Open row (787) covers only the tie-break.

**Why it matters:** The final audit is the win condition and the same number drives BMI (355). "Repair to full to wipe the attacker's credit" falls straight out of the wording and nobody has decided whether it is intended; and with no remainder rule two hosts can award different integer scores, so the cross-OS hash guard (680) binds nothing.

1. **Keep the proportional split; settle the five cases and the remainder in one sentence; exposure stays recap-only (RECOMMENDED)** — Line 261 gains: only damage dealt by other seats counts, clamped to the HP actually removed; reaching full HP clears the counters; an asset lost with no enemy damage on the clock — self-inflicted, terrain, units that disband, structures that become ruins — credits nobody; an eliminated seat's accrued share is dropped, not redistributed; integer apportionment by largest remainder, ties to the lowest seat id. Visibility unchanged: the live standings keep showing the total only, and the recap's "shared kill" line shows each seat's percentage for that asset. No live per-asset damage bar and no own-damage-share condition predicate; that goes to the roadmap. Section 15's determinism row names the per-seat counters and the remainder rule.
   *Downside:* "Repair to full erases the attacker's credit" becomes an explicit tactic a Build beacon can farm, and the recap has to teach it; the per-asset tally still cannot be seen or planned against during the Push, only read afterwards.

2. **Majority credit: most damage since last full HP takes the whole value** — Same case list as option 1, but no proportional split and no remainder rule at all: whichever seat dealt the most damage since the asset was last at full HP takes its entire build cost, shown in the recap as who took it. One rule a player can hold in their head, and one fewer determinism contract to write.
   *Downside:* A 51/49 attack pays the second seat nothing, which discourages joint pressure and can swing a close 3-way audit on a single shot; it reverses decisions-log 2.6 item 4; and a counter per seat is still needed to find the maximum, so little code is saved.

3. **Cut the split from v1: credit the killing blow** — Remove damage counters entirely. The seat that destroys an asset gets its build cost; an asset lost with no enemy involved credits nobody. No reset rule, no remainder rule, no per-asset state, and slice 2 loses its "damage-split kill credit" task (735). Revisit after the fun gate if vulture play shows up in the nightly league.
   *Downside:* Reinstates last-hit stealing, which draft 5 deliberately fixed and log 2.6 explicitly replaced; in a 3-way free-for-all vulture play is exactly the kingmaking behaviour section 3 already flags as a risk.

4. **Keep proportional and surface it** — Add a damage-share bar on known enemy assets in the Push view and the recap, plus one condition predicate for the seat's own share on a known enemy asset, so plans can finish what they softened.
   *Downside:* Grows slice 2 with UI and a new predicate family while the direction is to cut scope, puts arithmetic on screen during the movie against "accounting is not fun" (349), and risks leaking information about assets the seat can no longer see.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 keeps the decision already taken in log 2.6 and costs one sentence in section 3 plus one clause in section 15, while closing every undefined case and the determinism hole. It adds no UI, no predicate and no scope, and it answers the visibility question the cheapest way: the tally is the Ledger's arithmetic, reported once in the recap, not a live meter the player has to track.

**Spec edits:** Section 3, line 261: extend the credit sentence with the five cases (self-inflicted/unattributable, overkill clamp, disband and ruins, eliminated seat's share, full-HP reset) and largest-remainder apportionment; add that the recap's "shared kill" line shows each seat's share. Section 15 determinism row: name the per-seat integer counters and the remainder rule. Section 19 unchanged.

## 9. The audit rewards banking and punishes attacking [major] (§3, 5, 7, 9, 19)

**Evidence:** The final audit reads value as build cost, never condition. L261: "score = value held at the final tick ($ plus build cost of your beacons, structures and units) + build cost of enemy assets you destroyed" — so a 5%-HP beacon scores as intact, damage short of a kill pays nothing, and credit is only paid on destruction. L303/L370: "50% of the build cost is refunded" with no HP term, so a defender recycles a near-dead beacon for half full price and the attacker who spent an army gets zero. L353: "There is no $ upkeep" — banked $ cannot be destroyed, stolen or taxed. L264/L301: killing also pays salvage $ on top of the credit. L261 still asserts "Hoarding alone doesn't win."

**Why it matters:** A 6-round match is optimised against the audit, and as written it points at mine, defend minimally, never attack. That is the turtling the spec already fears (L297, L806), and a passive match is the worst case for the biggest unmeasured risk: an 8-minute Push with nothing happening.

1. **RECOMMENDED — Value follows condition (one rule, read in two places)** — An asset's value is build cost x current HP, wherever value is read: held value at the final audit and the recycle refund. Removal by recycle books the destruction credit exactly as destruction does, using the damage split already decided ("damage dealt since it was last at full HP"). $ stays at par, salvage stays as it is. Ledger story: it pays for what stands, in the state it stands in. Delete the unbacked line "Hoarding alone doesn't win" and let the existing fun-gate risk line cover hoarding.
   *Downside:* Banked $ is still the only riskless store of score, so the turtle-and-bank question is deferred to the nightly league and playtests rather than answered. The live standings number now moves during every skirmish, which is more visible jitter and one more sentence in the recap. Recycling a beacon that someone once chipped leaks a little credit to them, so damage counters must clear on full repair.

2. **Value follows condition, plus discount $ in the audit** — Option 1, and additionally $ counts at a fixed fraction of face value (e.g. 50%) at the final tick, in story "the Ledger does not count scrip it never saw spent". This closes the riskless-store hole outright instead of watching it.
   *Downside:* Adds a tuning number that silently sets the win condition, and it creates a final-Lull dump of treasury into cheap full-HP walls — exactly the accounting-flavoured optimisation the owner dislikes. It also hits the trailing seat that is saving up to rebuild.

3. **Minimal patch: scale only the recycle refund by HP** — Change one line in sections 5 and 7 — the refund is 50% of remaining value, not of build cost. Leave the audit exactly as drafted.
   *Downside:* Fixes the cash half of the recycle exploit only. Damage without a kill still scores nothing, a 5%-HP beacon still counts as intact at the final tick, free repair still restores full score, and the defender can still delete an attacker's whole investment and deny all credit.

4. **Change nothing; tune around it** — Keep HP-blind value, the flat refund and par $. Rely on the BMI leader penalty, the rank-weighted award fund and the fact that mining and salvage income need assets, and add hoarding to the section 19 risk line for the nightly league and fun gate.
   *Downside:* Carries a known dominant line (mine, minimum defence, never attack) through roughly 35 weeks to the fun gate, stacked on top of the no-control-movie risk it makes worse, and the spec keeps asserting something the rules do not deliver.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 is one multiplication applied in the two places value is already read: it needs no new number, no new ledger and no player-facing accounting, and it makes damage and repair legible in the number the standings already show. It also removes a rule (the flat refund) rather than adding one.

**Spec edits:** Section 3, L261: held value becomes build cost x current HP; extend the damage-split credit to removal by recycle; delete "Hoarding alone doesn't win". Section 5 L303 and section 7 L370: refund is 50% of remaining value. Section 9/19: state any seat's reclaim drones salvage any wreck, value small (Tuning). Section 19: add hoarding to the existing audit risk line.

## 10. When income lands, and what "audit" means per round [major] (§2, 3, 7, 12)

**Evidence:** §7 line 354: "Income per round Basic Minimum Income (BMI) plus awards. Mining and salvage add more." §7 line 356: "Decided Each audit, the Ledger releases a fixed award fund (tuned relative to BMI), split among that segment's winners in proportion to awards won." Nothing says when BMI or the fund actually lands, though the spec is precise elsewhere: mined ore is "credited to the seat treasury immediately" (337, 426) and recycle refunds pay in on commit (357). §3 line 261: "score = value held at the final tick". §3 line 253 puts "awards and 3 highlights" in every Recap. "Audit" also names two events: the recurring one at 356, and "the final audit" (223, 238, 261), the once-per-match score.

**Why it matters:** It decides the round-limit winner — whether the last round's BMI and awards are in the treasury at the final tick — and it is what a plan and get_economy_forecast need to know what is spendable during a Push. One word naming two events confuses players and docs.

1. **RECOMMENDED: one payout moment at the recap; "audit" means only the final audit** — At each segment end the Ledger settles in the recap: BMI plus the award fund are credited to the treasury, and the recap shows the line. During a Push the only income is mining and salvage, so the treasury is otherwise flat and predictable. The final audit is read after the last round's settlement, so "the final tick" becomes "after the final settlement". §7 stops saying "audit" and says "at each recap"; "the final audit" stays the only audit in the spec, UI and llms.txt. No new in-world term is added — one is removed.
   *Downside:* Anything a plan spends mid-Push must already be banked (or mined), so an aggressive Push plan cannot lean on income arriving during the segment; the recap gains one payout line before the score is shown.

2. **Credit at Push start instead** — BMI and the previous segment's award fund land as the seal closes and the Push begins, so the money is available for the segment the player just planned.
   *Downside:* Awards are computed at segment end but paid a round later, so the final round's awards are never paid while the final round's BMI is: the score systematically rewards income over performance in the last, most-watched round, and income now arrives at two different moments.

3. **No settlement after the final round** — Keep the pay-at-recap rule but skip it after the last round: the final audit simply reads the frozen segment-end snapshot as it stands, so no payout can distort the score and "value held at the final tick" stays true as written.
   *Downside:* The final round's BMI and awards are worth nothing, so award play collapses in the round that matters most, and players have to learn a last-round exception — an extra rule, not a simpler one.

**Recommendation:** One payout, at one moment the player already watches, keeps the treasury a number you can read rather than a rate you have to forecast — and dropping "audit" from §7 removes a term instead of adding one.

**Spec edits:** §7 L356: "At each recap, the Ledger settles: BMI plus a fixed award fund, split among that round's winners in proportion to awards won." L354 row: add "paid at the recap". §3 L253 Recap: add "Ledger settlement: BMI and awards paid". §3 L261: "value held at the final tick" → "value held after the final settlement". §12: bmi_next is "paid at the next recap". Keep "the final audit" as the only audit.

## 11. What survives a beacon's death [major] (§5, 6, 8, 9)

**Evidence:** L301: "Structures become unclaimed until an uncontested beacon of yours covers them, which absorbs them" — the spec's only use of "unclaimed". It never says whether such a structure supplies or draws kW, keeps its capability, can be repaired, counts in the audit ("build cost of your beacons, structures and units", L261), or can be salvaged (L333/429 name only "wrecks and ruins"), nor whose seat "yours" means. L358 treats all spheres as "one grid"; the overdraw ladder (L360) ignores unclaimed structures. Separately, re-home (L301), Recycle (L303) and the 8 s "Switch mandate type" (L318) hand a beacon units its mandate has no job for, while "Reassigning units comes in a later version" (L304). L382: "A destroyed structure suspends its capability until it is rebuilt" — who rebuilds is unstated; repair drones "fix its structures" (L333), its own beacon's only.

**Why it matters:** Killing a beacon is the game's most common raid. Whether it browns out a vent Generator, silences a network, or costs nothing is currently an implementation choice, and a mining drone under an Attack mandate has no defined behaviour or power draw.

1. **Delete the unclaimed state: dead beacon = local elimination, plus one fallback sentence for units and one for capability repair** — When a beacon dies its structures become neutral ruins immediately, exactly as on elimination (L264): inert, no supply, no draw, no capability, unrepairable, zero audit value, salvageable by any seat's reclaim drones. No absorption, no fourth state. Units still re-home (L301/303); add: a unit keeps its own role, and where the new or switched mandate has no job for it, it follows only the common settings (roe, retreat_hp_pct) and idles at the beacon — one sentence that covers re-home, Recycle and the 8 s mandate switch. A mandate switch clears the old mandate's settings and targets to defaults; the beacon's Quartermaster priority survives (already settled, L328). Capability structures: any of your Build beacons may repair any structure of yours in its drones' reach, but a destroyed one is never auto-rebuilt — it needs a fresh 5 s queue on site (L317).
   *Downside:* Losing a vent beacon instantly costs that Generator's whole output, so vent beacons become very fragile and the power swing is harsh; you also lose the tidy 'rebuild over lost ground and reabsorb your own orphans' story, and enemy reclaim drones can strip your base while you are still alive. The half-idle re-homed units still draw kW until recycled, which is a small chore.

2. **Keep absorption but define unclaimed as inert** — Unclaimed = no capability, no supply, no draw, unrepairable, counted at zero in the audit, salvageable by anyone like a ruin. "Yours" is stated to mean the original owner's surviving beacon (per decisions-log:516); re-absorption restores the structure in full. Units and capability repair as in option 1.
   *Downside:* Adds a fourth structure state to define, render, audit and expose in the API and verifier, and a race between the owner re-covering it and an enemy salvaging it — more rules to explain for a case that only arises after you rebuild onto lost ground.

3. **Unclaimed keeps running for its original owner until re-absorbed or destroyed** — Supply, capability and draw all continue; only repair and rebuild stop until an own beacon covers it again.
   *Downside:* Beacon raiding loses nearly all its economic teeth — a seat down to one beacon keeps a full power grid and capability network — and it quietly hands capabilities to a seat whose licensing beacon is gone, muddying both the 'one grid' and the licence stories.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 removes a state and a mechanic instead of specifying them: it reuses the elimination rule the spec already defines, ends the "unclaimed"/"no capture in v1" ambiguity outright, and leaves beacon raids the economic teeth the power design assumes — if the Generator swing proves too harsh that is tuning, not rules.

**Spec edits:** Section 5 Destroyed row: replace the unclaimed sentence with "its structures become neutral ruins" and drop absorption. Units row: add the keep-role / common-settings-only fallback and "a mandate switch clears that mandate's settings and targets; priority survives". Section 8: add "Repairable by any of your Build beacons in reach; a destroyed capability structure needs a fresh on-site queue and is never auto-rebuilt."

## 12. Commander: healing, respawn state, score, speed [major] (§3, 4, 6, 9, 10, 19)

**Evidence:** Four rules the commander is missing. Section 4 calls it "the only unit a seat drives directly" and "weak and killable" (282), moving at "a fixed speed" that is never stated (283). No healing rule exists: Build's drones "fix its structures" (333) and Defend's "damaged units fall back to the nearest friendly repair drone" (334) — the commander is neither. Death "respawns after a delay (for example 30 s, growing with each death)" (284), but whether the count carries is undefined against "Nothing carries over except the world itself" (442) and the snapshot "frozen at segment end" (272). The audit scores units and destroyed build cost (261, 264); the commander has no build cost anywhere. Section 19 Tuning lists "interface times; respawn delay" (802), no commander speed.

**Why it matters:** Draft 5 makes commander hunting a headline strategy with no stated cure, cost or payoff, and makes travel time the core currency with no number. Left open, week-4 movement code and week-17 map scale fix speed by accident, and a Lull snapshot may have no commander to route from.

1. **RECOMMENDED — one package: repairable like a unit, a fresh commander every Push, not an asset, speed named now** — Four clauses, one per gap. (1) Healing: repair drones repair the commander like any other friendly unit; no passive regeneration, no new mechanic, no new number. (2) Death: the death counter and its growing delay are per-Push state — they reset each segment, and a pending respawn always completes by segment end, so every Lull snapshot has a live commander at a known place and estimate_route, the "fits" pill and the verifier never model an absent commander. (3) Score: the commander is not an asset — no build cost, never in held value or destroyed-value credit, and it despawns at the tick its seat is eliminated, so the no-surviving-beacon respawn branch cannot arise; a kill pays only in the interface time the victim loses. (4) Speed: name commander speed as a world constant in section 4 with a starting range, express map scale and spawn separation in commander travel alongside raider travel, add a Tuning row, and confirm touches-per-Push at the fun probe (wk 17.5).
   *Downside:* A death in the last seconds of a Push is free — full HP plus a ride home — and hunting pays nothing on the scoreboard, so testers told commanders are "fair targets" may find a kill anticlimactic. Build beacons become the de facto medic, nudging every seat to field one.

2. **As recommended, but the death counter is world state** — The count and any pending respawn carry across segments. The snapshot reports "down, returns in t", and the editor and verifier draw a hatched gap at the start of the route, as the design doc already sketches. Keeps repeated deaths genuinely expensive and removes the free late-Push death.
   *Downside:* Adds an absent-commander case to the snapshot, travel estimator, "fits" pill, verifier and editor timeline — real work in slices 2 and 6 — and a hunted seat can lose most of a Push, which then needs its own UI warning.

3. **As recommended, but the commander counts in the audit** — Give it a notional build cost (for example the 2 rounds of BMI it starts with) so kills feed the damage-split credit like any other asset, and count the live commander in held value. Hunting then pays on the scoreboard and the nightly league can price it.
   *Downside:* Adds an accounting term to a score meant to stay simple, snowballs repeated kills on top of the growing respawn delay, and forces a further rule for whether a respawned commander re-adds its value.

4. **As recommended, but healing is passive regeneration inside your own powered spheres** — The seal's field patches the Keybearer: slow regeneration while inside one of your own lit spheres, and repair drones never touch the commander. Explains itself in the story and keeps Build drones scoped to structures and wrecks.
   *Downside:* One more tuning number and a commander-only special case in a spec that otherwise calls it a unit; it rewards hugging your own territory, and it weakens the Defend bodyguard and picket as the intended answer to hunting.

**Recommendation:** The first option is the only package that adds no new mechanic and no new score term — it reuses repair drones, and its per-Push counter deletes work, since nothing in the snapshot, estimator, verifier or editor ever has to represent a commander that is not on the map. Options 2–4 each swap exactly one clause of it, so you can mix.

**Spec edits:** Section 4: add the speed constant and range; add a Healing line (repair drones, no regeneration); rewrite Death for a per-Push counter, respawn always completing by segment end, and despawn on elimination. Section 3 final audit: the commander is not an asset (no build cost, held value or kill credit). Section 9 maps: state spawn separation in commander travel too. Section 19: add a commander speed Tuning row.

## 13. Nothing stops production or exhausts a seam [major] (§5, 6, 7, 9, 19)

**Evidence:** Two unbounded loops. Production: §5 (299) "There is no separate fabricator structure and no per-beacon unit cap"; §6 gives Build only "Fields repair/reclaim drones" (333) and Mine no count at all — "Seam choice: richest / nearest / safest; max dig depth; pillar spacing" (337); Defend/Attack unit_mix is minima only (gameplan doc 268). The only stop stated is §7 (361) "what its core surplus and vent Generators can power" and the Quartermaster (366) acting "when draw exceeds supply". Income: §9 (431) gives seams "varying richness" but never says one runs out; §19's tuning rows (799–800) cover BMI, costs, vents, salvage — no ore yield. Stale: gameplan doc 289 stop_at_pool assumes per-beacon pools that §7 (357) removed.

**Why it matters:** Read literally, every seat saturates its grid and parks at the brownout threshold: headroom is permanently zero, so the operator's headroom scoring (632) and the kW conditions (470) are dead, one Generator loss cascades, W06xx fires every round — and a safe rear seam mints income forever, making the contested centre optional.

1. **Finite seams, and fabricators build only for work in hand (RECOMMENDED)** — Digging consumes a seam's ore voxels: richness sets total yield, a spent seam goes visibly quiet, and "richest" means richest remaining. A beacon's fabricator requests a drone only while its mandate has outstanding work its current drones cannot keep up with (unbuilt or damaged targets, reachable ore), and stops when the work is covered. No new player-facing fields, no cap, same species of world rule as "each map has a finite number of vents" (358) and the unit ceiling as "a property of the world" (361). Headroom becomes real and mining pressure pushes outward over the match.
   *Downside:* The equilibrium is emergent, so one tuning number still has to exist (how much backlog buys another drone) and two engineers could read "work in hand" differently without it; late-match safe seams dry up and idle drones need a Ledger memo so it does not read as a bug.

2. **Finite seams plus explicit drone counts on Build and Mine** — Depletion as above, and the plan decides where production stops: a target drone count on Build and on Mine, and Defend/Attack unit_mix read as targets rather than minima. Templates pre-fill the numbers.
   *Downside:* Four or five more fields to set and to spend plan-size budget on, which is exactly the accounting the owner rules out; a field left at zero means no drones at all; and the defaults still have to answer the stopping question for every template user, so the real rule just moves into the template.

3. **Endless seams; a Quartermaster reserve keeps headroom** — State that seams never run out, so throughput and safety are the only mining limits, and have the Quartermaster leave a stated fraction of supply unspent on units so headroom always exists.
   *Downside:* Income stays unbounded, so risk-free rear banking is the strongest line and the map's richer central seams are never worth contesting; the reserve is a hidden number players feel but cannot see, and it is an artificial cap in everything but name, needing its own story justification under the caps rule (198).

**Recommendation:** Option 1 answers both halves with one world rule and zero new fields, so it removes scope rather than adding it, and depletion is what makes the generator's richer central seams worth fighting over instead of decorative.

**Spec edits:** §9: seams are finite, richness sets total yield, spent seams read as spent; §6 Mine: "richest" means richest remaining, still no counts. §7: add a row — fabricators produce only for outstanding mandate work, so headroom is the normal state. §19: tuning rows for ore yield per richness and the backlog threshold. Drop stale stop_at_pool.

## 14. When $ leaves the treasury [major] (§3, 5, 7, 10, 11)

**Evidence:** Sec 7 says where money comes from, never when it leaves: "All spending (fabricators, setup charges, licensed structures) draws from it through the Quartermaster" (357). Sec 5 creates an in-progress state anyway: "Deploying takes 12 s" (298), "Queue a licensed capability structure 5 s, plus fabricator construction time" (317), and recycle pays "50% of the build cost is refunded when it commits" (303). Sec 3's win condition values "value held at the final tick ($ plus build cost of your beacons, structures and units)" (261) without saying what a half-built one is worth; the damage-split kill credit inherits the same hole. Sec 11 blesses "$ and kW projection (Quartermaster arithmetic)" (524) as an estimate. The supporting doc assumes a reserve model ("reserves $", plan_spend_reserved); the spec never says it.

**Why it matters:** The audit is the win condition and the Quartermaster projection is a verifier-blessed estimate, so both need one rule. Charge-on-completion lets a seat queue far more than it can afford; zero-value-while-building makes seats stop building before the final tick. Identical play can produce different winners.

1. **RECOMMENDED - Paid means yours: charge in full at commit, full build-cost value from that instant** — $ leaves the treasury the moment the order commits (the place_beacon deploy starts, the structure is queued, the unit is ordered). From that instant the asset sits on your books at its full build cost for every purpose: the final audit, the live standings, the 50% recycle refund, and the damage-split kill credit if an enemy kills it before it finishes. There is no partial value anywhere in the game: construction time is HP and spectacle, not accounting. Nothing is refunded if it dies mid-build. If the treasury cannot cover an order, the order fails like any other step failure (on_fail / skip_if), and the existing economy family (E0601 added draw, W06xx shortfall) covers the plan-time error and the tight-forecast warning.
   *Downside:* A raider gets full build-cost credit for destroying a structure that has barely started, which can look generous on camera; and because orders drain the treasury the instant they commit, a plan whose big spend comes second can find the money already gone and silently lose its main action.

2. **Charge at commit, but an unfinished asset is worth nothing** — Same single charge moment, but a beacon still deploying, a structure still building or a unit still in the fabricator counts zero in the audit, refunds nothing, and gives its killer no credit. Value appears only when the asset completes.
   *Downside:* Your score visibly dips every time you start building and only recovers on completion, so the optimal play near the final tick is to stop spending - accounting-driven behaviour of exactly the kind the owner rules reject. Raiding a construction site also earns the attacker nothing, which contradicts the intent of the damage-split credit.

3. **Charge on completion** — Nothing unfinished has a cost, a value or a credit; the treasury is debited only when the thing exists.
   *Downside:* Breaks the verifier rule that adding draw beyond supply is an error at plan time, makes get_economy_forecast meaningless, and lets a seat queue far more than it can afford and leave the Quartermaster to sort it out.

4. **Charge progressively with build progress** — The treasury drains as construction advances; an unfinished asset is worth the fraction paid, in the audit, the recycle refund and the kill credit.
   *Downside:* Exactly the per-tick accounting the owner rules out, and the progress fractions have to surface in the audit, the recap, the standings and get_economy_forecast - four more places to get right for no gain in play.

**Recommendation:** It is the only option with no second state to track: money is either spent or not, an asset is either on your books at full cost or does not exist, and the audit, the forecast, the recycle refund and the kill credit all read the same number.

**Spec edits:** Sec 7, new Spending row: "$ leaves the treasury when the order commits. From that moment the asset counts at its full build cost - audit, recycle refund and kill credit alike. There is no partial value and no refund if it dies mid-build. An order the treasury cannot cover fails (E0601 family)." Sec 3 line 261 and sec 5's recycle row cite build cost regardless of completion.

## 15. Plan execution: pre-emption, the reflex, atomic edits [major] (§5, 6, 10, 13)

**Evidence:** Section 10 leaves three linked holes. (1) Pre-emption: line 440 orders each 250 ms decision "reflex first (commander HP ≤ 20%, which can't be disabled), then the first enabled rule whose condition is true, then the current step", and line 441 covers only "When a rule fires: it aborts the remaining changes of any visit in progress". Nothing says whether rules are scanned while a rule body is running — and the sample body holds 8000 ms (line 494). (2) The reflex is named twice (440, 464) but its action is never stated; section 6 line 332 uses "Reflexes (retreat, return fire)" for a different mechanism. (3) Line 309 says "Changes commit one at a time, in order" while line 315 prices "2 s + 0.5 s per extra field", so a five-field edit is one commit or five — and line 614 draws "a tick at each change's commit point".

**Why it matters:** This is the interpreter's core contract. Two engineers build opposite machines from the same paragraph, the editor's worst-case detour lane and commit ticks cannot be drawn, and the P1 per-rule tick cost that sets the plan size budget has no defined worst case.

1. **RECOMMENDED — Reflex is the only interrupter; bodies atomic; interface rows atomic** — Three sentences in section 10. (a) At most one rule body runs at a time, and no rule may pre-empt another; only the self-preservation reflex interrupts anything. (b) The reflex fires at HP ≤ 20% (fixed, not disableable): it aborts any visit, walks the commander to the safest own beacon, and the route then continues from the step it was on; it can fire again only after the commander takes new damage. Drop options.reflex_hp_pct from the schema — an author who wants a braver or more timid commander writes an ordinary flee rule, which the sample plan already does at 40%. (c) Each row of the interface table is one change that commits at the end of its own duration, so a multi-field settings edit is all-or-nothing; a resumed visit is a new visit and pays the handshake again. Also rename section 6's unit-level 'reflexes' to return fire so the word means one thing.
   *Downside:* A flee rule cannot cut short a long rescue detour, so a commander can keep walking into fire until it hits 20%; lowering the reflex to 10% is no longer possible; an interrupted 6 s settings edit loses every field, which pushes cautious players to split edits across rows.

2. **Restore the co-design contract in full (preempt flag + tunable reflex)** — At most one body at a time; a rule above the running one and flagged pre-empting may take over; the pre-empted body is dropped, not resumed, and the new rule's resume policy applies. Reflex action as above but with a threshold field, default 20, lowerable to 10. Interface rows atomic as in option 1.

## 19. What the human can author, and in how long [major] (§1, 3, 10, 13, 16, 19)

**Evidence:** Line 170: "Humans, LLM agents and the built-in AI plan with the same schema and pass the same verifier." Nothing is ever stripped, and the expert vocabulary survives for v1.1 scripts without editor work.
   *Downside:* Needs a submit path gamectl does not have today, splitting the human's workflow in two; keeps interpreter and verifier scope for constructs no v1 tool authors; and a player who hand-writes one branch can never open that plan in the editor again.

3. **Keep the schema, editor preserves unrenderable nodes as locked read-only rows** — The editor gains a passthrough model: constructs it cannot render survive Load and Save as locked rows showing their rendered sentence, with inverse-patch undo working across them and golden round-trip tests byte-comparing files that contain them.    *Downside:* The most slice-6 work of the three (passthrough plus undo across opaque nodes), it keeps every branch/repeat cost in sim, verifier and schema, and locked rows are the expert view by another name — the thing that was deliberately cut to Roadmap.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 is the only one that removes scope rather than adding it, and it converts an undefined silent-data-loss path into a normal verifier diagnostic; with agent seats now v1.2 there is no v1 consumer of the full-power vocabulary to protect.

**Spec edits:** S10: drop set_flag, branch, repeat and flag predicates; keep the field numbers reserved. S11: E03xx shrinks. S13: state "the v1 schema is what the editor renders; Load rejects anything else with a diagnostic, never strips"; pre-load last round's plan as an editable, re-verified draft. S3: delete the no-reuse sentence. S16: P6 becomes template ≤2 min, scratch ≤5 min. S19: drop the sophistication risk row.

## 30. What the verifier gates actually measure [minor] (§11, 12, 13, 16)

**Evidence:** Two loose ends in one place. (a) Depth is invisible in the API. Line 529: "decode → structure → resolve → semantics (together called QUICK, under 5 ms, run on every edit) → estimate → lint (FULL, under 50 ms, always run on submit)". Line 530 calls the report "a pure function of plan bytes, snapshot, rules hash and verifier version" — depth is not in that list, though the two depths produce different reports. Line 558 lists "verify_plan" with no depth argument, while line 550 says the editor "runs no validation or time maths of its own" and line 619 has it running both depths. (b) The gate measures an undefined quantity. Line 709: "≤50 ms p99 at the size budget; editor re-check ≤16 ms" — "editor re-check" is never defined (QUICK itself? QUICK + estimate_route + redraw?).

**Why it matters:** P1 sets the plan size budget and the per-rule tick cost. A gate on an undefined quantity can be passed by a build that misses the 5 ms promise, and an unstated depth makes the report_hash equality claim unprovable — it compares reports that may not be of the same depth.

1. **RECOMMENDED — Name depth; gate the verifier only, not the editor frame** — verify_plan takes depth: quick | full, default full; submit always runs full. Add depth as the fifth input in the determinism sentence and say report_hash is comparable only between reports of the same depth. In P1 replace "editor re-check ≤16 ms" with the two verifier numbers it already owns: "QUICK ≤5 ms p99, FULL ≤50 ms p99 at the size budget". Editor typing feel stays a P6 concern, not a CI number.
   *Downside:* verify_plan gains a parameter on the tool the spec most wants to keep small, and nothing in CI guards the editor's end-to-end keystroke latency until P6 — a slow Godot round trip could ship unnoticed even with a 5 ms verifier.

2. **One depth: verify_plan always runs FULL; drop QUICK from the API** — Delete the two-depth split from section 11 and section 13: the editor re-verifies after 600 ms idle (as line 619 already says) and shows nothing verifier-side between keystrokes. QUICK survives only as build order — S0–S3 land in the skeleton, S4–S5 in slice 1. The determinism sentence's four inputs then become correct as written and the 16 ms line goes. P1 gates one number: FULL ≤50 ms p99.
   *Downside:* Removes per-keystroke error highlighting, a real editor affordance; errors appear ~0.6 s after typing stops, and the whole editor loop then rides the 50 ms budget with debounce as its only fallback if the size budget grows.

3. **Keep both numbers and define the 16 ms** — Define "editor re-check" in section 13 as the whole keystroke round trip — QUICK plus estimate_route for changed legs plus redraw — and gate it at ≤16 ms, one frame at 60 fps, alongside QUICK ≤5 ms and FULL ≤50 ms. Add the depth parameter as in option 1.
   *Downside:* Three thresholds in one gate, and the 16 ms one needs a headless Godot timing harness in CI from the skeleton onwards — added CI scope, and it can fail for redraw reasons that have nothing to do with the verifier.

**Recommendation:** Option 1 keeps the behaviour that already exists, costs one parameter and one clause, and removes CI scope by letting the verifier gate measure only the verifier; option 2 is the pick if draft 6 narrows toward v0.1 and per-keystroke checking is not worth a second depth.

**Spec edits:** S11 L529–530: add depth as a fifth determinism input; note report_hash compares only within one depth. S12 L558: verify_plan{depth: quick|full, default full}; submit always full. S16 L709: replace "editor re-check ≤16 ms" with "QUICK ≤5 ms p99; FULL ≤50 ms p99 at the size budget"; editor latency moves to P6.
